* Goal
Make "auto" picture quality react to the room. v0.3.0 stopped punishing a
lagging viewer; it did nothing to stop them lagging, because auto is still
dash.js's throughput ABR with no idea that a stall or a catch-up just
happened. After this plan: a stall or entering catch-up caps the viewer one
rung below what they were playing, a clean minute lifts the cap one rung,
every viewer starts on the 720 rung and climbs, and the ladder's bitrates
drop to what a residential uplink behind a quick tunnel can actually serve
to a room.

Decisions taken (Nathan, 2026-10-16): step down on stalls *and* on entering
catch-up; step up after 60 s clean; ladder 1080p 4000k, 720p 2000k, 480p
900k, 360p 450k with an env override; start at the 720 rung.

* Findings
- Auto is dash.js alone. =player/set-auto= flips
  =streaming.abr.autoSwitchBitrate.video= and nothing else
  (~src/cljs/goob_toob/player.cljs:25-26~).
- dash.js 4.7 has the knobs. =streaming.abr.maxBitrate.video= and
  =streaming.abr.initialBitrate.video=, both in *kbps*, =-1= for none
  (~node_modules/dashjs/dist/dash.all.debug.js:3667-3690~). The cap is
  enforced in =getMaxAllowedIndexFor= on every quality decision
  (~dash.all.debug.js:24450~), so changing it mid-stream takes effect at the
  next segment; =fastSwitchEnabled= is already on. dash.js clamps
  =initialBitrate= to the highest representation at or below it, so a 476p
  source with a single 360 rung still starts on that rung.
- Quality indices ascend by bitrate. =getQualityFor "video"= returns the
  playing index; =getBitrateInfoListFor "video"= gives ={qualityIndex,
  height, bitrate (bps)}= in that order; =player/video-representations=
  re-sorts by height descending for the menu (~player.cljs:18-23~).
- The signals exist on the client. Stall entry is the =onwaiting= handler
  that sets =buffering= (~src/cljs/goob_toob/watch.cljs:212~); catch-up entry
  is =apply-policy!= moving =mode= to =:catch-up= (v0.3.0). Neither touches
  quality.
- Ladder is a constant with no override: =config/default-ladder=
  (~src/clj/goob_toob/config.clj:11-13~), 6000/3000/1200/600 kbps. In desktop
  mode every viewer pulls their own stream through the tunnel from the host's
  uplink, so three viewers on 1080 is 18 Mbit/s upstream today, 12 after.
- The welcome message carries what the player needs to start
  (~src/clj/goob_toob/ws.clj:53-59~); the start bitrate goes there.

* Plan
** 1 · sync_core.cljc: quality-cap, cap-kbps, start-kbps (pure)
#+begin_src clojure
(def quality-tuning
  {:step-up-ms  60000
   :min-step-ms 10000})

(defn quality-cap
  "state is {:cap idx :at ms} or nil for no cap. event is :stall, :catch-up
  or :tick. cur is the quality index dash.js is playing, top the highest
  index (both ascend by bitrate). Returns the new state; nil means no cap.
  A stall or catch-up caps one rung below the playing rung, at most once per
  :min-step-ms; a :tick after :step-up-ms clean lifts the cap one rung and
  clears it at the top."
  [state event cur top now]
  (let [{:keys [step-up-ms min-step-ms]} quality-tuning]
    (case event
      (:stall :catch-up)
      (if (and state (< (- now (:at state)) min-step-ms))
        state
        {:cap (max 0 (dec (if state (min cur (:cap state)) cur))) :at now})
      :tick
      (cond
        (nil? state)                          nil
        (< (- now (:at state)) step-up-ms)    state
        (>= (inc (:cap state)) top)           nil
        :else                                 {:cap (inc (:cap state)) :at now})
      state)))

(defn cap-kbps
  "reps ascend by quality index with :bitrate in bps. -1 means no cap."
  [state reps]
  (if state (quot (:bitrate (nth reps (:cap state))) 1000) -1))

(defn start-kbps
  "The 720 rung's bitrate, else the lowest rung's."
  [ladder]
  (:v (or (first (filter #(= 720 (:h %)) ladder)) (last ladder))))
#+end_src

  #+begin_src clojure
  (let [ctx (.-player st)
        reps (player/quality-list ctx)
        next (sc/quality-cap (.-qcap st) ev (player/current-quality ctx)
                             (dec (count reps)) (ui/perf-now))]
    (when (not= next (.-qcap st))
      (set! (.-qcap st) next)
      (let [kbps (sc/cap-kbps next reps)]
        (player/set-max-kbps ctx kbps)
        (log! token :quality-cap {:event ev :cap (:cap next) :kbps kbps}))))
  #+end_src

* Risks and open questions
- *Edge-triggered events can double-fire.* A stall and the catch-up it
  causes arrive seconds apart; =:min-step-ms= (10 s) makes the second a
  no-op. If real stalls cluster faster than that on bad links, the cap
  still only moves one rung per 10 s, which is the intended pace.
- *The cap is advisory to dash.js.* It bounds the ABR's choice; dash.js may
  still sit below the cap on its own throughput estimate, which is fine, and
  =quality-cap= steps down from the *playing* rung, not the cap, so a
  viewer dash.js already dropped is not pushed two rungs further.
- *A locked resolution bypasses all of this* by design; the cap is cleared
  when the viewer locks and when they return to auto (fresh start).
- *Old transcodes keep the hot ladder.* Nothing migrates existing DASH
  trees; re-upload to get the new bitrates. The README says so.
- *=initialBitrate= is start-only.* Reloading the page restarts at 720; the
  cap state is not persisted. Deliberate: a reload is the viewer's reset.
